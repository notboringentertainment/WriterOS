# Story Bible

From the writer's story-bible best-practices template. Explain the creative rules and how the story sustains its format, rather than expanding a synopsis.

- Cover and identity: title, writer, format, genre, version and date when known.
- One-page pitch: logline, premise, hook, central question and audience promise.
- Tone and style: comps, pacing, dialogue, visual language, sound and limits.
- World: locations, rules, power structures, history and mythology that generate conflict.
- Characters: wants, needs, flaws, secrets, contradictions, relationships, voice and arcs.
- Story engine: central conflict, repeatable episode engine, future seasons and endgame; for a feature, propulsion and transformation.
- Pilot and season map: opening, major turns, episode events, character changes, world reveals and future setup.
- Continuity log: established and locked facts with sources, changes and open questions kept distinct.

Read active canon, Story Facts, the other pages and the beat sheet. Mark missing decisions rather than inventing them. This text never changes canon without the writer's explicit yes.
